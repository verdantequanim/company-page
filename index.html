What HeadWater is

HeadWater is a chronic stress survey for adults. It takes about 12 minutes on a phone or a computer. A text message version is in progress. It asks about stress, sleep, mood, work and money, food, the place you live, early life, substance use, and the people around you. It is anonymous. Nobody sees a name with an answer.

HeadWater is designed to show each person plain language feedback by area when they finish, and where to find help nearby. That results screen is being built now. The question about thoughts of self harm shows the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. A full crisis screen with local contacts is being built.

HeadWater is not a diagnosis and not a medical device. It does not tell anyone they have a condition. It shows a person where their stress load sits and gives them somewhere to start.

What the Stress Atlas is

The Nebraska Stress Atlas is designed to combine survey results by county, with no names and with small counties pooled so no one can be identified. It is built to show a county, a hospital, a clinic, or a health department where chronic stress is concentrated, so they can decide where to put their effort.

It is designed to grow into a map of which evidence based practices a county already has against the load it carries, and of the upstream drivers of what is coming.

HeadWater survey layers on the public Atlas open county by county once a county passes the respondent floor and the data governance board approves release. Partners and reviewers can ask for access to the survey layers.

Where we are

The first version of the survey was tested with 86 adults in Nebraska in early 2026. Reliability was measured for each subscale separately and is reported that way. The survey is live on the web and on Android, with an Apple version in progress. The Atlas shows public health data for all 93 counties today. Survey results are added county by county as they are approved for release.

In 2027, with rural partners across Nebraska, we plan to run the survey in rural counties, report results to each partner and to the state, and measure whether the subscales hold in a rural sample before anything more is built on top of them.

How we handle data

We never sell a person's answers. Person level data is released to no one. Combined, de-identified county results may be licensed to partners the data governance board approves, for uses it approves, under contracts that forbid re-identification and resale. The board reviews what leaves the platform before any results are published.
